World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia, Ukraine and the US opened day two of peace talks in Abu Dhabi, the first trilateral talks since February; Marco Rubio called day one "very productive", with no breakthrough as Russia kept hitting power sites (Reuters). Leaders addressed the COP31 summit in Antalya, where Turkey wants electricity at 35% of global energy by 2035; the US stayed away for a second year (AFP). The Associated Press called Mississippi's Senate race for Democrat Scott Colom, who beat incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith by 4,669 votes; she conceded, lifting Democrats to 55 seats (AP). Millions struck in Spain in its first general strike in 14 years, over housing costs, 18 days before a 29 November snap election (AP). The House voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, shelving it; only sponsor Rep. Thomas Massie broke ranks (AP). A Florida judge let President Trump's $10bn BBC defamation suit proceed over an edited Panorama clip, dismissing a trade-practices claim (BBC). The US Justice Department charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot against RAF Fairford in Britain, linked to nearly 20 attacks, prosecutors said (BBC). Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases, with 5,387 deaths, the government said (AP). El Salvador claimed its troops in Haiti destroyed the 400 Mawozo gang's HQ; the BBC could not verify the video, and a reported toll of 20 was unconfirmed (BBC). Trump said the FBI was examining whether Nvidia chief Jensen Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence, a claim China called baseless (Reuters). Anthropic set a $70-$78 price range for its Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, aiming to raise up to $64.4bn (CNBC). Brent crude rose to $92.20 a barrel after attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, including a tanker fire off Oman (Reuters). Asian shares mostly rose after Wall Street fell on hot US October inflation; Tokyo's Nikkei up 0.8%, Seoul's Kospi up 1.6% (AP).
