World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

Russia, Ukraine and the US opened a second day of peace talks in Abu Dhabi, the first trilateral meeting since February; US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called day one "very productive", but there was no sign of a breakthrough, and Russia kept striking Ukrainian power infrastructure overnight (Reuters). World leaders began addressing the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, where host Turkey is pushing a goal of raising electricity's share of global energy to 35% by 2035; the US stayed away for a second year (AFP). Millions of workers struck across Spain in its first general strike in 14 years, demanding action on housing costs 18 days before a snap election on 29 November (AP). In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively shelving the effort; only Rep. Thomas Massie, its sponsor, broke ranks (AP). A Florida judge let President Donald Trump's $10bn defamation suit against the BBC proceed over an edited Panorama clip, dismissing a separate trade-practices claim (BBC). The US Justice Department charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed plot against RAF Fairford airbase in Britain, linked by prosecutors to nearly 20 attacks or attempted attacks (BBC). President Trump said the FBI was examining whether Nvidia chief Jensen Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence, a claim China called baseless (Reuters). Anthropic set a $70-$78 price range for its Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, aiming to raise up to $64.4bn (CNBC). Oil rose, with Brent crude at $92.20 a barrel, after attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, including a tanker fire off Oman (Reuters). A bus crash in Seongnam, South Korea, during morning rush hour injured 57 people, nine seriously (Yonhap).
